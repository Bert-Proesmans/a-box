# Error Handling & Failure Modes — Implementation Plan

## Blueprint

[[13-error-handling-failure-modes]] is unusual among this project's specs: almost everything it describes is *emergent behavior* of mechanisms other, already-planned components build for their own reasons, not a standalone thing to construct. Launch-time atomicity and stop cascades are a direct structural consequence of [[10-session-lifecycle-orchestration-plan|10's]] `.target`/`BindsTo=`/`Requires=`/`PartOf=` unit graph (capstone-proved generically at [[10-session-lifecycle-orchestration-plan#Step 2.6 — Full unit-graph atomic-launch and stop-cascade proof|Step 2.6]]); two of this spec's three stop-cascade triggers are [[11-session-transcript-receivers-plan|11's]] growth-cap and idle-watchdog mechanisms; the fatal and best-effort failure examples this spec classifies are [[12-production-hardening-plan|12's]] hugepage-pool-exhaustion and `kvm-pit`-poststart failures respectively; and BPF-violation handling is explicitly unchanged from [[07-bpf-monitoring#Violation response policy|07's log+alert-only policy]]. None of that mechanism is rebuilt here.

What genuinely still needed checking: whether `launch`'s failure-surfacing behavior — "`launch` surfaces the failing unit's `systemctl status`/journal output to the user; nothing is retried automatically" — was actually built anywhere. It was. [[10-session-lifecycle-orchestration-plan#Step 3.1 — `launch`|10's plan Step 3.1]] states outright: "On failure of step (7), surface the failing unit's `systemctl status`/journal output to the user, and explicitly release any slot this launch's own `ExecStartPre=` may have acquired." So this plan adds **no implementation step** for failure-surfacing — only a verification step confirming that behavior actually manifests for a real, not synthetic, failure.

What this plan does add: integration-test-style verification steps that prove the emergent behaviors actually hold once all the pieces they depend on are real, not placeholders. Each of 10/11/12's own plans proved their own mechanism piecewise, often against placeholder `ExecStart=` scripts or in isolation from the other two plans' real content (10's Step 2.6 induces a placeholder helper's non-zero exit, not a real bind failure; 11's Steps 1.1/2.3/4.2 each prove one cascade trigger individually, never all three back to back; 12's Step 3.2 builds the `kvm-pit` script but never demonstrates against the full real graph that its failure is actually non-fatal). This plan's two chunks close that gap: Chunk 1 proves launch-time atomicity, failure-surfacing, and the fatal/best-effort classification against the fully-assembled real graph (post-11, post-12), using the concrete failure examples [[13-error-handling-failure-modes|13's own spec text]] and [[14-testing-strategy|14's testing strategy]] name by name (an already-bound vsock path, hugepage exhaustion, `kvm-pit` placement failure) rather than 10's placeholder stand-ins. Chunk 2 proves all three stop-cascade triggers converge on an identical teardown outcome in one consolidated run, rather than three separately-proved but never cross-checked mechanisms.

Given how much of this spec is already built elsewhere, this plan is deliberately small: one chunk of two steps, one chunk of one step.

**Open gap check.** Re-reading [[15-decisions-log]] fresh: its "Still open" section now contains exactly one entry, "Resource limits → systemd unit property mapping" (relates to [[12-production-hardening]]'s cgroup-knob-to-unit-directive mapping) — not directly relevant to anything in [[13-error-handling-failure-modes]]. This plan flags one gap of its own instead, found while grounding Step 1.2 below: [[12-production-hardening-plan#Step 3.2 — `kvm-pit` cgroup placement `ExecStartPost=` script|12's Step 3.2]] builds the `kvm-pit` poststart script and specifies it should log three distinguishable outcomes, but neither that step nor [[13-error-handling-failure-modes|this spec]] states whether the script's own exit code is engineered to never fail the unit (e.g. via systemd's `ExecStartPost=-` failure-ignoring prefix, or an internal catch-all `exit 0`) — a detail that must hold for "best-effort, non-fatal (logged, session proceeds)" to actually be true under real systemd semantics, since an ordinary `ExecStartPost=` failing *does* fail the unit. Flagged at Step 1.2, verified empirically there rather than resolved by reading 12's implementation source.

## Chunks

1. **Chunk 1 — Launch-time failure behavior: atomicity, surfacing, and fatal-vs-best-effort classification.** Proves launch atomicity and `launch`'s failure-surfacing against the real (non-placeholder) unit graph using 14's named vsock-bind-conflict scenario, then proves the fatal/best-effort classification concretely using real hugepage exhaustion and a real `kvm-pit` failure. (Steps 1.1–1.2)
2. **Chunk 2 — Stop-cascade convergence.** Proves all three independent stop triggers (manual stop, wall-clock timeout, receiver/watchdog self-stop) converge on an identical teardown outcome in one consolidated run. (Step 2.1)

## Step 1.1 — Real-graph launch-atomicity proof and failure-surfacing verification

Builds on [[10-session-lifecycle-orchestration-plan#Step 2.6 — Full unit-graph atomic-launch and stop-cascade proof|10's Step 2.6]] (which proved the atomicity mechanism generically, against a placeholder helper's induced non-zero exit) and [[10-session-lifecycle-orchestration-plan#Step 3.1 — `launch`|10's Step 3.1]] (which already implements failure-surfacing — nothing to build there). By this point [[11-session-transcript-receivers-plan]] has replaced every helper-unit placeholder with real content (`recv-bpf` real at its Step 1.1, the stdio bridge/terminal tap real at its Steps 2.1–2.3, the idle watchdog real at its Step 4.2) and [[12-production-hardening-plan#Step 2.2 — Wire jailer argv into the VM unit: `Delegate=yes`, `--cgroup-version 2`, `IPAddressDeny=any`|12's Step 2.2]] has replaced the VM unit's placeholder with a real jailer/firecracker invocation — this is the first step where a launch failure can be induced against the genuine graph instead of a stand-in.

```text
Context already built, not to be rebuilt here: a complete per-session systemd unit graph with every unit's real content in place — agentvm-session@<id>.target wrapping a VM unit that execs real jailer/firecracker (not a placeholder sleep script), three BindsTo=+After= helper units (a real recv-bpf receiver enforcing a 100 MB cap, a real session_manager.py stdio bridge/terminal tap enforcing the same cap, and a real recv-proxy relay), and an idle-timer pair running a real mtime-based silence check — all Restart=no, all PartOf= the VM unit, with the whole set proven to start as one atomic systemd transaction and to cascade down together on any single trigger. Separately, the launch CLI subcommand already: counts active session-target units against the configured concurrency cap before doing anything else; on any failure of the systemctl start call, surfaces the failing unit's systemctl status/journal output to the user and releases any slot acquired before the failed transaction. This failure-surfacing behavior is already implemented — this task does not add it, only proves it fires correctly against a real failure.

Specification facts to ground this task in: a session's unit graph starts as a single systemd transaction; BindsTo=/Requires=-family dependencies mean a failure in any required unit (the spec's own named examples include "a receiver failing to bind its vsock UDS path") fails the whole systemctl start agentvm-session@<id>.target transaction, leaving no orphaned half-started session and no manual cleanup path to write or maintain. launch surfaces the failing unit's systemctl status/journal output to the user; nothing is retried automatically. This project's own testing-strategy note names the exact scenario to prove this with: inject a failure in one required unit (e.g. an already-bound vsock path) and confirm the whole target fails to start with no leftover running units.

Task: write a needs_kvm integration test (a standalone runnable test module — no shared test-marker infrastructure exists yet, and building that is a separate, not-yet-planned component's job; mark this test needs_kvm in a plain comment/docstring per this repo's naming convention without building the marker mechanism itself) that:

1. Launches one real session via the launch CLI and confirms it reaches active cleanly (a sanity baseline against the real, fully-wired graph).
2. Stops it, then deliberately pre-occupies the recv-bpf helper's dedicated vsock UDS path for a fresh session id — bind and hold open a Unix domain socket at exactly the path the real recv-bpf unit would bind for a new launch, before calling launch for that new session id — reproducing the spec's own named "already-bound vsock path" failure.
3. Calls launch for that new session id and confirms: the whole agentvm-session@<id>.target transaction fails to start; querying systemctl for every unit in that session's graph (target, VM unit, all three helpers, idle timer pair) shows none left active — no orphaned half-started session; the concurrency slot this launch attempt may have begun acquiring is not left held (query the slot allocator's own state); and launch's own CLI output names the specific failing unit (recv-bpf) and includes that unit's systemctl status/journal content distinctly — not a generic failure message — proving the already-built failure-surfacing behavior actually manifests for a real bind failure, not just in the code path's own logic.
4. Releases the held socket, retries launch for the same session id, and confirms it now succeeds — proving the failure left no residue behind to block a subsequent, valid launch.

If step 3's surfacing check reveals the CLI output is generic rather than naming the specific failing unit, treat that as a real bug in the already-built failure-surfacing code and fix it minimally so the specific-unit content is included — do not silently accept a weaker result, and do not build a second, parallel surfacing mechanism alongside the existing one.

Verify: run the test and confirm all four stages produce the outcomes described above. This closes the loop on both of [[13-error-handling-failure-modes]]'s "Launch-time atomicity" section and the "Launch-atomicity" needs_kvm scenario named in the project's testing-strategy note, against the real graph rather than a placeholder stand-in.
```

## Step 1.2 — Fatal-vs-best-effort failure classification proof

Builds directly on Step 1.1's real-graph atomicity proof and on two pieces of [[12-production-hardening-plan]]: the hugetlbfs pool and its own launch-time preflight guard ([[12-production-hardening-plan#Step 1.1 — Hugepages: static pool sizing at host boot|Step 1.1]], [[12-production-hardening-plan#Step 1.3 — Firecracker machine-config `huge_pages` wiring|Step 1.3]]), and the `kvm-pit` cgroup-placement poststart script ([[12-production-hardening-plan#Step 3.2 — `kvm-pit` cgroup placement `ExecStartPost=` script|Step 3.2]]). This step gives concrete, real-failure evidence for one of each of this spec's two failure classes, using the exact examples the spec itself names.

```text
Context already built: Step 1.1's proof that a real required-unit failure (an already-bound vsock path) fails the whole launch transaction atomically, with launch surfacing the specific failing unit's status. Separately, from a different plan: a statically-sized, boot-verified hugetlbfs pool (250 MiB x max_concurrent_sessions, in 2M pages) with a launch-time preflight guard that refuses a launch cleanly if the requested guest RAM looks larger than the pool's own accounting believes is available — and that same plan's own text documents a standing, unresolved operational risk: the pool's real kernel-level size and the CLI config's max_concurrent_sessions value are set together by hand, with no automatic sync, so the two can drift. Also already built: a kvm-pit cgroup-placement ExecStartPost= script on the VM unit that locates the kvm-pit kernel thread and migrates it into the unit's delegated cgroup, logging three distinguishable outcomes (found-and-moved, not-found-within-poll-window, or move-failed).

Specification facts to ground this task: not every failure should block a launch or kill a session. Fatal (fails the launch transaction): hugetlbfs pool exhaustion, which surfaces as a Firecracker API/InstanceStart error. Best-effort, non-fatal (logged, session proceeds): the kvm-pit cgroup-placement poststart script failing to find or move the thread — this affects CPU-accounting precision only, not correctness or isolation, and is explicitly flagged in the spec itself as a decision to revisit if empirical testing shows the placement is reliable enough to be a hard requirement instead.

An open gap to verify rather than assume: for the kvm-pit case to actually be non-fatal under systemd, the poststart script's own exit code must not fail the unit (either the script must itself always exit 0, or it must be wired with the ExecStartPost=- failure-ignoring prefix). Neither the spec nor the plan that built the script states this explicitly. Do not resolve this by reading that script's implementation source directly — resolve it empirically, by observing whether a real induced failure in that script actually leaves the session running or not, and report which is true as this task's own finding.

Task: write two needs_kvm(+needs_root, since this touches real cgroups/hugepage state) integration tests, run back to back:

(a) Fatal — real hugepage exhaustion. Rather than tripping the existing launch-time preflight guard (which refuses cleanly before ever reaching Firecracker, a softer and already-distinct failure path), reproduce the plan's own documented drift risk directly: shrink the real kernel-reported hugetlbfs pool (e.g. temporarily lower the running pool's available page count) to below what a new session's guest RAM requires, without changing the CLI config's own accounting, so the preflight guard's belief and the kernel's real state disagree. Launch a session under this condition and confirm: the real jailer/firecracker invocation's own InstanceStart-equivalent call fails for lack of hugepages; the whole launch transaction fails atomically, with the identical no-orphaned-units and slot-not-leaked signature Step 1.1 already established for the vsock-bind-conflict case; and launch's failure-surfacing output includes the real Firecracker-level error text, not a generic message. Restore the pool afterward and confirm a subsequent launch succeeds normally.

(b) Best-effort — real kvm-pit placement failure. Induce the poststart script's own thread-migration step to fail for real (for example, by making the delegated cgroup's cgroup.procs file temporarily unwritable for the duration of that one poststart invocation only). Launch a session under this condition and confirm: the poststart script logs its move-failed outcome distinctly; the VM unit itself still reaches active and the session proceeds fully usable (guest boots, is reachable, behaves identically to a session where the script succeeded) — this is the concrete proof, or disproof, of this spec's "session proceeds" claim. If the VM unit instead fails to start as a result of the script's failure, do not patch the script from within this test-writing task — report this as a confirmed real gap (the ExecStartPost= exit-code contract question flagged above) rather than silently working around it.

Verify: run both scenarios and confirm the fatal case matches Step 1.1's atomic-failure signature exactly, and the best-effort case leaves the session fully functional with only a log entry — or, if it does not, that this plan's flagged gap is now a confirmed finding rather than a hypothesis. This closes Chunk 1: [[13-error-handling-failure-modes]]'s atomicity, failure-surfacing, and fatal/best-effort classification are now proven against the real system, not a placeholder stand-in.
```

## Step 2.1 — Stop-cascade convergence proof across all three triggers

Builds on [[10-session-lifecycle-orchestration-plan#Step 2.6 — Full unit-graph atomic-launch and stop-cascade proof|10's Step 2.6]] (which proved manual-stop and RuntimeMaxSec-timeout cascades generically, against still-partly-placeholder helpers) and [[11-session-transcript-receivers-plan#Step 1.1 — `recv-bpf`: real receiver wiring & cap-cascade proof|11's Step 1.1]], [[11-session-transcript-receivers-plan#Step 2.3 — Real-graph wiring, attach-state independence, and cap-cascade proof|11's Step 2.3]], and [[11-session-transcript-receivers-plan#Step 4.2 — Idle-watchdog real wiring & stop-cascade trigger|11's Step 4.2]] (each of which proved one cascade trigger individually, against the real receiver it belongs to, but never cross-checked against the other triggers in the same run). This step is the first place all three independent triggers this spec names are exercised back to back against the identical real graph.

```text
Context already built: a fully real per-session unit graph (as in Step 1.1/1.2 above) where each of the following has already been individually proven, in its own originating plan, to cascade down the whole graph: a manual systemctl stop on the target; the VM unit's own RuntimeMaxSec= wall-clock timeout expiring; the real recv-bpf receiver hitting its 100 MB cap and exiting; the real terminal-transcript tap hitting its own 100 MB cap and exiting; and the real idle-timer watchdog firing after 10 minutes (overridable to a short threshold for testing) of combined silence across all three transcript streams.

Specification facts to ground this task: three independent triggers converge on the same mechanism — the unit graph's BindsTo= relationship, VM unit to the two receivers plus stdio bridge. Manual stop; the session wall-clock timeout; and a receiver or the stdio bridge stopping on its own (whether from hitting its cap, the idle watchdog stopping it deliberately, or an unrelated crash) all converge on "the VM unit stops," because BindsTo= doesn't distinguish why a bound unit went inactive. This is a deliberate fail-closed posture: an unrelated bug in a small receiver can take down a whole agent session, which is exactly why those receivers are kept minimal and well-tested in the first place. This task's job is to prove the convergence itself — that all three trigger categories really do produce an indistinguishable outcome from systemd's own point of view — not to re-prove any one trigger's mechanism, which is already done.

Task: write one needs_kvm integration test harness that, against freshly-launched real sessions (one per scenario, not a shared session reused across scenarios, so no scenario's teardown interferes with another's setup), exercises all five concrete triggers this spec's mechanism covers, in sequence:

1. A manual stop on the target.
2. A short RuntimeMaxSec= timeout expiring on its own.
3. The real recv-bpf receiver's 100 MB cap being hit by driving real guest BPF-event volume past the threshold.
4. The real terminal-transcript tap's 100 MB cap being hit by driving real terminal output past the threshold.
5. The real idle-timer watchdog firing after a short overridden silence threshold with no activity on any of the three transcript streams.

For each of the five, capture and compare: the final systemd state of every unit in that session's graph (target, VM unit, all three helpers, idle timer pair) — all must end inactive; whether any process from the session's graph is still running at the OS level after the cascade completes — none must be; and whether the session's concurrency slot is released (per the existing ExecStopPost= slot-release wiring) — it must be, in every one of the five cases, regardless of which unit initiated the stop. Assert that the five outcome signatures are identical to each other in every one of these respects — not merely that each individually reaches "stopped," but that a caller inspecting final state after the fact cannot tell which of the five triggers caused it, which is the concrete, checkable form of this spec's own claim that BindsTo= doesn't distinguish why a bound unit went inactive.

Verify: run the harness and confirm all five scenarios converge on the identical teardown signature described above. This closes Chunk 2 and this plan: [[13-error-handling-failure-modes]]'s stop-cascade section is now proven as an actual cross-trigger convergence, not five separately-plausible mechanisms that happen to share a dependency structure.
```
